Personal Care Assistant & Respite Care for Autism Families: A Step-by-Step Guide to Medicaid and OPWDD Services

Published: June 8, 2026 Reading Time: 18 min Topics: PCA, Medicaid, OPWDD, Respite, CDPAP

Caring for a family member with autism is one of the most demanding and rewarding commitments a family can make. New York State offers two critical programs — Personal Care Assistant (PCA) services through Medicaid and Respite care through OPWDD — that can provide real, meaningful support. Many families don't know these services exist or believe they are too complicated to access. This guide walks you through both programs step by step — from eligibility to your first day of service.

Part 1: Personal Care Assistant (PCA) Through Medicaid

Medicaid-funded PCA services provide hands-on, in-home support for individuals with autism who need help with daily activities.

What Is a Personal Care Assistant?

A Personal Care Assistant (PCA) is a trained caregiver who comes to your home to assist an individual with autism in their daily life. For someone on the autism spectrum, a PCA can provide:

Daily Living Support

Bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, and meal preparation — hands-on help with the tasks of everyday life.

Behavioral Support

Supervision, redirection, and support during behavioral challenges. Keeping the individual safe and engaged.

Community Integration

Accompanying the individual to appointments, activities, and community outings they could not manage alone.

Caregiver Relief

When a PCA is present, family caregivers can work, rest, handle errands, or simply breathe.

Who Qualifies for PCA Services?

To receive PCA services through New York State Medicaid, the individual must:

  • Be enrolled in New York State Medicaid
  • Have a documented disability that requires assistance with daily activities — autism spectrum disorder qualifies
  • Require help with activities of daily living as determined by a home care assessment
  • Live in the community (not in a hospital, nursing home, or residential facility)
  • Have a physician's order supporting the need for home care

Don't Have Medicaid? Apply First.

PCA services are funded through Medicaid. If the individual with autism is not enrolled, apply before anything else. Apply online at ny.gov/services/apply-medicaid or visit your local Department of Social Services (DSS). Children with disabilities often qualify regardless of family income through the Medicaid Buy-In program.

CDPAP — The Game-Changer for Autism Families

The Consumer Directed Personal Assistance Program (CDPAP) is a Medicaid-funded alternative to traditional agency PCA services that gives families maximum control. Under CDPAP:

  • The individual with autism (or their parent/guardian) chooses their own caregiver — someone they already know and trust
  • A family member, friend, or neighbor can be hired and paid as the PCA (parents can qualify; spouses typically cannot)
  • YOU train the caregiver in exactly how your family member needs to be supported
  • A Fiscal Intermediary (FI) handles payroll, taxes, and paperwork — you focus on care

Why CDPAP Matters for Autism Families

Many individuals with autism experience significant distress when cared for by unfamiliar people. CDPAP solves this by allowing the family to hire someone already trusted by the individual — dramatically reducing behavioral challenges and improving quality of care. If agency-provided PCA has not worked for your family, CDPAP may be the answer.

To apply for CDPAP: Contact your Medicaid managed care plan and ask specifically to be enrolled in CDPAP. You will then choose a Fiscal Intermediary (FI). The state's primary FI as of 2025 is Public Partnerships LLC (PPL): 1-833-247-5346 | publicpartnerships.com

Step-by-Step: How to Get PCA Services

1

Confirm or apply for Medicaid enrollment

Check your Medicaid status or apply at ny.gov or your local DSS office. If already enrolled, identify which Medicaid Managed Care Plan covers you (look at your Medicaid card).

2

Call your Medicaid Managed Care Plan

Call the member services number on the back of your Medicaid card. Say: "I am requesting a home care assessment for Personal Care Assistant services for my family member with autism." Ask specifically about CDPAP if you want to choose your own caregiver.

3

Complete the in-home nursing assessment

A registered nurse from the managed care plan or a home care agency will visit your home. They assess functional needs and determine the number of PCA hours to authorize. Be thorough and specific about all the tasks the individual needs help with — describe the worst days, not the best.

4

Get a physician's order

Your doctor or specialist must provide a written order supporting the need for PCA services. Contact your child's developmental pediatrician, psychiatrist, or primary care physician. Explain you are applying for Medicaid PCA services and need an order.

5

Choose traditional PCA or CDPAP

  • Traditional PCA: The plan assigns you a licensed home care agency that employs the PCA
  • CDPAP: You choose your own caregiver and select a Fiscal Intermediary (FI) to manage payroll
6

Services begin

Once the plan of care is approved, your PCA or CDPAP worker begins. Hours are reviewed regularly. If your family member's needs increase, request a reassessment — you can always ask for more hours.

NY Medicaid Managed Care Plans That Cover PCA Services

If you are unsure which plan you have, check your Medicaid card or call 1-800-541-2831 (NY Medicaid helpline).

Part 2: Respite Care Through OPWDD

OPWDD-funded Respite gives family caregivers a break — and gives individuals with autism supported time in the community or a safe environment outside the home.

What Is OPWDD?

The New York State Office for People With Developmental Disabilities (OPWDD) is the state agency that coordinates services and supports for individuals with developmental disabilities, including autism spectrum disorder. OPWDD is separate from Medicaid managed care — it has its own eligibility process, its own service system, and its own funding stream.

OPWDD services include residential programs, day programs, employment support, community habilitation, and — critically for families — Respite care.

What Is Respite Care?

Respite is temporary, planned relief for the primary caregivers of an individual with autism. It is not a reflection of failure — it is a clinically recognized support that helps families remain intact, healthy, and sustainable as caregivers over the long term.

In-Home Respite

A trained support worker comes to your home. The individual stays in their familiar environment while caregivers get time to rest, work, or manage other responsibilities.

Out-of-Home Respite

The individual stays at a licensed respite facility, camp, or family care home for a short period — a few hours, overnight, or up to 14 days at a time.

Community-Based Respite

A support worker takes the individual into the community — activities, outings, social programs — giving caregivers a break while the individual builds independence.

Crisis Respite

Emergency short-term placement when a family caregiver is hospitalized, incapacitated, or facing an immediate crisis. Can be arranged rapidly through OPWDD.

Who Qualifies for OPWDD Services?

To receive OPWDD-funded services, including Respite, an individual must:

  • Have a qualifying developmental disability — autism spectrum disorder qualifies
  • The disability must have originated before age 22
  • Be a New York State resident
  • Have documentation of the disability (diagnostic evaluations, medical records, school records)

No Income Limit for OPWDD Eligibility

Unlike some programs, OPWDD eligibility is based on the presence of a qualifying disability — not family income or assets. However, many OPWDD services are funded through Medicaid, so Medicaid enrollment is often needed to access funded services. OPWDD can help families apply for Medicaid if needed.

Step-by-Step: How to Access OPWDD Respite

1

Contact the OPWDD Front Door

The "Front Door" is your starting point for all OPWDD services. Call: 1-833-OPWDD NY (1-833-679-3369)
Or find your local Developmental Disabilities Regional Office (DDRO) at opwdd.ny.gov/contact
You can also start online at opwdd.ny.gov/front-door

2

Gather your eligibility documents

OPWDD will need documentation to determine eligibility. Prepare:

  • Formal autism diagnosis from a licensed psychologist or physician (evaluation report)
  • Medical records showing the diagnosis and history
  • School records (IEP, psychological evaluations from school)
  • Proof of NY State residency
  • Birth certificate or proof of age
3

Attend your Front Door intake meeting

OPWDD staff will meet with you and your family (in person or by phone/video) to explain available services, review your documentation, and begin the eligibility determination process. This meeting is free and carries no commitment.

4

Receive your eligibility determination

OPWDD will review your documentation and issue a written eligibility determination. If approved, you are officially in the system and can begin accessing services. If denied, you have the right to appeal.

5

Get connected with a Care Manager

A Care Manager (employed by a voluntary agency, free to you) becomes your guide through the OPWDD system. They help you understand what services are available, connect you with providers, and develop your Individualized Service Plan (ISP). Request a Care Manager immediately after eligibility is confirmed.

6

Develop your Individualized Service Plan (ISP)

Working with your Care Manager, you create an ISP that outlines all the supports and services your family member needs — including Respite. Be specific: type of respite (in-home, out-of-home), frequency (weekly, monthly), and hours per month. The ISP is reviewed annually and can be updated anytime needs change.

7

Choose a Respite provider and begin services

Your Care Manager will provide a list of OPWDD-approved Respite providers in your county. You can interview providers and select the one that is the best fit for your family member. Once selected, Respite services begin according to your ISP.

OPWDD Self-Direction — Maximum Control for Families

Similar to CDPAP in Medicaid, OPWDD's Self-Direction program allows families to design and manage their own supports — including hiring and managing their own Respite workers from their personal networks.

  • You choose WHO provides the support — a trusted person in your family's life
  • You set the schedule that works for your family's routine
  • A Support Broker guides you through the process (free)
  • A Fiscal Intermediary manages payroll and compliance
  • You maintain a budget and make decisions about how funds are spent

Ask your Care Manager specifically about Self-Direction when developing your ISP. More information at opwdd.ny.gov/self-direction.

Can You Have Both PCA (Medicaid) and Respite (OPWDD)?

Yes. These are separate programs with separate funding streams. A family can receive PCA hours through Medicaid for daily care AND Respite hours through OPWDD for caregiver relief. They serve different purposes and are not duplicates. Your OPWDD Care Manager and Medicaid managed care plan can coordinate to ensure both work together.
